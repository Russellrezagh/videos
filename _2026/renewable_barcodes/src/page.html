Writing history as it happens

A single DNA barcode labels a lineage, but it is inherited by every descendant, so later mutations inside an expanding lineage disappear from view. Renewable barcoding solves this by inserting a fresh barcode at the same locus every 100 generations, using the Cre recombinase and a set of one-way lox sites.

Each cell ends up carrying a string of barcodes that reads like a family tree. Reading those strings over 1,000 generations let the authors resolve thousands of competing lineages far below the limit of genome sequencing, and watch the distribution of fitness move as a travelling wave.
