Counting evolution as it happens

Part one explains how a laboratory evolution experiment works: a clonal ancestor, a challenging environment, serial batch transfers every 48 hours, and a frozen fossil record. Part two follows the Levy et al. (2015) barcoding system, where a random 20-nucleotide tag is stitched into each yeast genome by Cre–lox recombination, so that sequencing can track every lineage in a population of 10⁸ cells.

Part three follows Kinsler, Geiler-Samerotte & Petrov (2020), who used the same barcodes to measure 292 adaptive mutants in 45 environments and found them locally modular yet globally pleiotropic.
