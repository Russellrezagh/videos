- In chess, both players see every piece. Nothing about the state of the game is hidden.
- In Stratego, you see where the enemy pieces stand, but not their ranks. Each player arranges forty hidden pieces.
- Both players know the rules, so the information is complete. But the state is hidden, so the information is imperfect.
- One player can arrange the pieces in about ten to the thirty-three ways. You cannot list them, and you cannot search them all.
- Here is a small version of the problem. Your hidden piece is a General, with probability one quarter, or else a Scout.
- The General always advances. The Scout advances with probability b. That advance is a bluff.
- The opponent sees an advance, but not the rank. By Bayes' rule, more bluffs make a General less likely.
- So the value of a bluff depends on how often you bluff. A move has no fixed value of its own.
- At equilibrium, the Scout bluffs about thirty-eight percent of the time, and the opponent stands about twenty-nine percent of the time.
- Ataraxos learns by self-play. Each policy moves toward the actions that score well against the other.
- Without control, the two policies chase each other in circles. They never settle.
- Ataraxos damps each update. A step size, eta, limits how far the policy moves. A pull, alpha, holds it near a random magnet.
- The new policy is a weighted mix of the old policy, the magnet, and the exponential of the action values.
- If the pull is too weak for the step, the path keeps circling. With a strong enough pull, it settles.
- Ataraxos reduces the pull and the step together. Then the path walks to the equilibrium, and exploitability goes near zero.
- In a match, a belief network samples possible arrangements of the hidden pieces, given everything seen so far.
- For each sample, the search plays a thousand short games with the policy, and averages the result of each move.
- Then it makes one damped update of the network policy. A leash, beta, keeps the result near the network.
- Without the leash, the search trusts noisy estimates, and the rating falls by almost five hundred Elo.
- In July 2025, Ataraxos played Pim Niemeijer, the world number one. It won fifteen games, drew four, and lost one.
- Training cost less than eight thousand dollars. That is about one five-hundredth of the cost of DeepNash.
- The same method also beats top humans at Barrage Stratego, sets a new best score in Hanabi, and beats the best dou dizhu programs.
- The lesson: when information is hidden, control the learning dynamics, and treat search as one more careful update.
