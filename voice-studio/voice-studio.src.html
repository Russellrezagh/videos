Voice and English study · for a Persian speaker in the US

Cumberbatch Voice Studio

Built for thinking in English without translating from Persian, widening your vocabulary, using chunks in the right context, and handling "what if" with confidence, all with his rhythm and sentence craft. Voice targets are measured from the reel (106 s, his reading of Rik Mayall's Band Aid letter, Letters Live 2024) and 20 minutes of his unscripted podcast talk.

Room 1 · The instrument

What his voice actually does

Two modes of the same speaker. On stage he sprints and stops. In conversation he thinks out loud, steadily. You'll train both, because the stage tricks only sound natural once the conversational base is solid.

Pace across the reel

words per minute in 10-second windows · teal bars are silences of a second or more

He swings between 96 and 228 words per minute. The slow windows are where the jokes land and he waits for the laugh. The fast ones are the story logistics, which he hurries through on purpose.

Ten melodies worth stealing

Pitch in semitones relative to his median (158 Hz). Above the line is higher than his usual voice. Learn the shape, not the words: hum each one, then put your own sentence on it.

Room 2 · Think in English

From Persian to English, without the translation step

Translating in your head is slow because it's two jobs: find the Persian thought, then rebuild it in English. The goal is one job: situation in, English out. Six habits get you there. Below them are the traps Persian sets for English, so you can fix each one once and stop worrying about it.

Room 3 · Chunks and words

Ready-made English for real moments

Chunks are grouped by what you're trying to do, and many show the Persian phrase you'd reach for in the same moment. Cards give you the situation and ask you to produce the English. The Persian cue fades out as a card climbs its boxes. Ten new cards a day.

Room 4 · Sentences

Sentence shapes that carry you

Fluent speakers don't build sentences word by word. They pull ready-made frames and pour content in. Fill a frame out loud five times a day and it becomes a groove your mind slides into on its own. Start with the everyday structures, then add his.

Two worked examples
Your situation

Grow a sentence

He rarely says a short, flat sentence. He grows it: a better word, a stack, a soft hedge, a clause, an afterthought, then a short landing. Before each reveal, try the move yourself out loud.

Room 5 · What if

Hypotheticals, without hesitating

Everyday Persian lets one sentence shape cover both the imaginary present and the imaginary past. English makes you choose, and that choice is where the hesitation comes from. Learn one question and the forms follow.

Room 8 · The ring

Unscripted, against the clock

This is where it becomes yours. You get a situation, one required frame and three words. Talk for the full time without stopping. Silence is allowed; "um" is not. Replace every filler with a pause or a discourse marker.
